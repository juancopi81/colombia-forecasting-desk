My recommendation

Build a private Colombia research brief with two reader uses: investment research and civic understanding. Start with three continuing questions that match the evidence already collected:

  1. Are financing conditions becoming easier or tighter? Connect inflation, central-bank decisions and government borrowing costs. Explain implications for credit-sensitive businesses and fiscal funding, with explicit uncertainty.
  2. Where is economic activity strengthening or weakening? Follow production, retail, imports and exports by period and sector. Turn the evidence into questions about demand, costs and business exposure.
  3. What changes in public money and household rules? Track material budget, tax, pension and public-service changes from proposal through adoption and implementation. Explain who is affected and what takes effect when.

Civic explanations and macroeconomic research are the strongest immediate uses. Security-specific investment decisions need another layer: issuer filings, business exposure, balance sheets, valuation and a relevant time horizon. Daily currency and share-price moves alone do not supply that layer.

The reader gets

  • A brief of at most 350 words, with up to three material changes.
  • Plain names before bill codes or acronyms.
  • What changed, why it matters, who is affected, what remains uncertain and the next useful action.
  • One weekly synthesis showing how the continuing stories have changed.

The research process gets

  • One persistent record per important question, with a dated view and next trigger.
  • Research time allocated to the most consequential missing evidence.
  • Forecast questions and baselines prepared before their release or vote windows.
  • Full source evidence and validation available behind the short brief.

Use the strongest model to compare versions, test explanations, synthesize a continuing case and judge materiality. Delegate bounded extraction and verification tasks when they save work. Continue using deterministic code for collection, dates, arithmetic and recordkeeping.

First implementation slice: add the short reader brief and three continuing case records to the existing workflow. Then test them over the next ten weekday runs. Keep forecast selection and publication separate.

Success: the brief is understandable in under two minutes; on material days, you can name a belief, investigation or decision it informed; repeated stories show progress or are explicitly parked. A quiet day is acceptable. If this still fails to help, narrow or stop that topic. Forecast counts are not a quota.

Evidence and implementation detail

Why the current process produces this experience
  1. The daily job rewards process completion. The active automation explicitly asks for counts, separate internal/public labels, follow-up receipts and the original 10-run pilot status. Its repeated pilot retrospective follows the prompt even though that pilot was already reviewed in August.
  2. The main schema has no explicit reader-use test. The analysis contract requires an interpretation, alternatives, falsifiers and citations. It does not require an affected audience, material consequence or reader action. The generated lead contract asks for a research next-check, which is a different thing.
  3. The dashboard gives operational details first. At a 1280-pixel browser width, October 27 rendered at 2,938 pixels wide and 15,184 pixels tall. “Top analyst insights” starts around 7,669 pixels down. The earlier intelligence section contains authored findings, but mixes them with extensive references and review metadata.
  4. Two meanings of “insight” compete. The dashboard’s top insights come from generated screens: a construction-cost gap and a small procurement sample. The latter itself says more checking is needed before treating it as an insight. The authored tobacco finding sits elsewhere.
  5. Important research gaps keep recurring. In all 19 October observations, the expectations survey source failed and the policy/IBR and labor cards failed extraction. Some underlying facts were recovered manually elsewhere, so this is not total absence of policy or labor information. Overall scan acceptance is insufficient to qualify a particular research question.
  6. Research is organized around each day’s scan. Existing packs and monitor queues provide some continuity, but there is no concise reader-level account of what changed in a case, what it now means, and why it deserves further work. A fixed three-source/ten-minute daily follow-up is useful triage; complex legal or investment cases can need a separately budgeted deeper investigation.
  7. Learning is sparse. Fifty-six structured analysis days produced four internal forecasts. Waiting for ten additional resolved forecasts before another qualitative review can postpone a usefulness review for a long time. Product review needs its own calendar.

These are product-design judgments grounded in the artifacts, not proof that a different model or looser forecast gate would have predicted the missed events. The October 12 manufacturing abstention was a substantive assessment with mixed evidence, not simply an unfinished check.

Small implementation plan and what to preserve
  1. Reader brief: add a small authored record containing a plain title, material change, consequence, affected audience, evidence/inference distinction and next action or trigger. Render it first; put technical diagnostics in expandable sections. The same brief should supply the chat summary.
  2. Case continuity: start with three cases, keeping stable identities, dated updates, outstanding evidence and closure/reopening conditions. Avoid repeatedly starting research from a daily snapshot. Preserve historical forecasts and cutoffs.
  3. Focused preparation: qualify one repeatable macro forecast family early, with a stable metric, comparison and resolver. Record a probability only when the judgment is defensible. Explicitly distinguish uncertainty about an outcome from uncertainty about what the question means or how it resolves.
  4. Repair by decision value: prioritize a source/parser when it blocks an active case, such as policy/expectations for monetary research. Do not expand collection simply to improve record counts.
  5. Evaluate usefulness: record whether each substantive brief changed understanding or prompted a worthwhile check, how much reading it required, whether follow-up closed, and research cost. Assess forecast performance separately when enough outcomes exist.

Preserve explicit sourcing, uncertainty, human publication review, immutable forecast timestamps and criteria, and the rule that missing records never resolve an outcome. No broad rewrite, database migration or new model dependency is needed for the first slice.
